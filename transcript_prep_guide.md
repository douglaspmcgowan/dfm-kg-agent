# Transcript Preparation Guide for Qualitative Coding

---

## 1. Speaker Attribution

- Verify all speaker labels match the actual names used in the study — fix any intro misstatements early
- Assign every floating/unattributed line to a speaker before coding
- Check that all interviewers are represented throughout — if one is silent for long stretches, verify against audio that their turns weren't missed
- Watch for turns where one speaker's line bleeds into another's, especially follow-up questions that look like continuations

---

## 2. Filler Word Bracketing

Bracket all filler words and phrases so coders can choose to include or exclude them consistently. Apply brackets to:

- [like]
- [you know]
- [um]
- [I mean]
- [I don't know] — when used as a filler, not a genuine statement of uncertainty

**Decision to make before coding:** decide once whether bracketed fillers are included or excluded as data units, and apply that decision uniformly across the whole transcript.

---

## 3. Proper Nouns and Platform Names

- Correct capitalization and full names of tools, platforms, and brands (e.g. "hey orca" → "Heyorca", "sprout" → "Sprout Social")
- Flag intentional lowercase brand names (e.g. monday.com) so coders don't treat them as errors
- Standardize AI/Ai → **AI** throughout

---

## 4. Duplicated or Garbled Text

Common transcription artifacts to find and fix:

- Duplicated phrases (e.g. "Thursdays and Fridays are Thursdays and Fridays are")
- Duplicate words mid-sentence from false starts (e.g. "and so it's a this thing")
- Mis-transcribed proper nouns or industry terms (e.g. "dog going head sound" → "going heads down")
- "Watercooler" commonly transcribed as "water break water room" or similar

---

## 5. Verify Against Audio

Flag any of the following for audio review before finalizing:

- Lines marked [inaudible]
- Passages that are grammatically broken in ways that suggest missing words
- Any exchange that seems abruptly cut off
- Lines where the speaker attribution feels wrong
- Interviewer questions that appear mid-answer (may indicate overlap or interruption not captured)

Use **[VERIFY AGAINST AUDIO]** inline at the exact location of the problem.

---

## 6. Structural Issues for Coding

- **Section headers** added editorially should be clearly marked as such — do not code them as participant data
- **Multi-part interviewer questions** in a single turn should be split into separate turns so each answer can be cleanly coded to a specific question
- **Leading questions** (e.g. numeric scale prompts) should be flagged in a note so coders can account for interviewer influence on the response
- When a long run-on closing or transition passage contains multiple distinct topics, split it into separate turns

---

## 7. End of Recording

- Mark the end of the recording explicitly: **[end of recording]**
- Note any cut-off sentences at the end and whether they were resolved against audio

---

## 8. Editorial Notes Section

Include a short notes section at the bottom of the transcript for coders, covering:

- Any name standardization decisions made
- Bracketing conventions used
- Unresolved [VERIFY AGAINST AUDIO] flags that remain
- Any structural decisions (split turns, removed duplicates) that changed the original
